High-Value Talks

Detailed first-principles writeups for the talks that best frame the summit: frontier robotics ambition and deployment reality.

Talk 9 · NVIDIA

Jim Fan / NVIDIA: Robotics - The End Game

Today a robot sits idle most of the day because it learns only from someone teleoperated controlling it for 3 hours. We need robots to learn like Tesla learns to drive—from millions of hours of human video without needing a robot on-site—then use world models (models that predict what pixels come next) to ground that knowledge into robot actions.

Talk 36 · Field AI

Ali Agha / Field AI: Deployment Over Demos

A robot sent to a construction site or oil refinery to carry material over unmapped terrain—with no GPS, no cleaned paths, and no second chances for mistakes—must be built on architecture that bakes in physics awareness and safety checks, not just neural-network predictions.

Talk 8 · Google DeepMind

Carolina Parada / Google DeepMind: Language to Motion

A robot can understand a natural-language request like 'sort these fruits into color-matching plates' and figure out the step-by-step movements needed, combining a reasoning layer that plans what to do with an action model that produces the actual arm motions.

Talk 18 · Lightwheel

Steve Xie / Lightwheel: Continuous Learning Systems for Physical AI

Imagine a robot arm in a factory that starts weak but keeps improving: it watches humans fold packages, practices the task thousands of times in a simulation, fails in predictable ways, and uses real deployment feedback to get better week after week—that's the continuous learning loop physical AI needs.

Talk 19 · Stanford and Centific

Jiajun Wu / Stanford and Vasu Sundarababu / Centific: Physical AI's Data Gaps

When you watch a robot fold clothes or pack a box, it's seeing video but not feeling pressure, weight, or contact—and there's no common way to collect or combine these different sensor streams so robots can learn from real-world mistakes the way humans do.

Talk 34 · Rhoda AI

Andrew Wooten / Rhoda AI: World Action Models

Imagine a factory unpacking thousands of boxes of bearings by hand. A robot arm struggling with the task because it only saw teleoperated examples, not how plastic deforms or transparent materials behave. Rhoda AI's approach: pre-train the robot's "world model" (its understanding of physics) on millions of hours of internet video, then teach it the specific task with just 10 hours of robot video, so it can predict what the task looks like frame-by-frame and convert those predictions into real robot commands.

Talk 20 · Samsara and Fraikin

Samsara and Fraikin: Physical AI Deployment Gap

A truck's engine throws an error code on a motorway in France. Traditionally, the driver calls the shop, waits for a work order, parts are ordered with delays, and the vehicle loses a day. With AI agents watching all 60,000 vehicles in a fleet every second, the system detects that error code, automatically creates a work order, orders the part before the driver even calls, knows when the part arrives at the nearest workshop, and routes the truck there so it's fixed within hours—no human touches the decision, and one error triggers a coordinated cascade from telemetry to workshops across an entire continent.

Talk 31 · ANYbotics, Nomagic, Sunrise, and Schaeffler

ANYbotics, Nomagic and Schaeffler: Robots at Work

When a warehouse robotic arm finally picks beans fast enough, the customer asks for 2x the speed; when a quadruped navigates an oily aluminum factory floor, its foot sensors corrupt because decades of accumulated oil wasn't in the training data; when a radar gets tripped by a flapping trash bag, the robot halts—these are the real failures that make robotics companies iterate from production, not labs.

Talk 38 · Dynamic Creatures, McKinsey, TRON, AGIBOT, and Humanoid

Dynamic Creatures, McKinsey, TRON and AGIBOT: Building a Robotics Business

A robotics company wins not by selling impressive machines, but by finding real problems where a robot makes money for the customer—and preparing their organization to actually use it reliably.

Talk 41 · Bright Machines, Roboto AI, Kinisi Robotics, and Kitemaker

Bright Machines, Roboto AI and Kinisi Robotics: Factory Deployment Gap

You build a robot that works in the lab, ship it to a factory, and suddenly discover the real work is not the robot itself—it's integrating it into an existing operation, handling the data it produces, maintaining it in the field, and keeping the customer happy when they demand 50% faster performance than what you tested.
